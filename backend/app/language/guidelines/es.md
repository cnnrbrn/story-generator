# Guidelines: Spanish history-story video scripts (4 levels)

These are guidelines for video scripts for teaching Spanish through history stories at 4 different levels.

## Story levels

There are 4 story difficulty levels:

- Just Starting
- Beginner
- Intermediate
- Advanced

## Structure / section headings

- Title in Spanish
- Title in English
- Summary in English
- Vocabulary from the story
- Verbs from the story
- The story in Spanish
- La gramática de la historia (no para grabar)

### Section headings

At intermediate level all section headings must be in Spanish. So "Vocabulary from the story" becomes "Vocabulario de la historia", "Verbs from the story" becomes "Verbos de la historia", "Now, the story" becomes "Ahora, la historia", and "La gramática de la historia (no para grabar)" stays as is.

At Advanced level there is only the title in Spanish and the story in Spanish, no other sections.

## Summary section rules

- Written for native English speakers, no need to simplify language.
- Keep it short and precise.
- Use specific language rather than vague descriptions.
- Each level should have a slightly different summary even if the content of the story is very similar.
- Summaries do not need to get more complex at higher levels, they just need to be good and reflect what is in the Spanish story.

## Vocabulary section rules

- Only include words that are genuinely difficult or non-obvious.
- Do not include cognates.
- Do not repeat words from previous level vocabulary sections, e.g. if `duro` is covered in Just Starting do not include it in the Beginner version of the same story.
- Do include contractions like "del" (in Just Starting or Beginner), numbers in Spanish.

## Verbs section rules

- Each entry: verb - translation. Example sentence taken exactly from the story. English translation of example.
- In Just Starting and Beginner levels, include all verbs that appear in the story.
- New verbs should appear more than once in the story where possible.
- At intermediate level, do not repeat verbs from previous levels in the verbs section, only introduce new ones.
- All verbs in the story regardless of level appear in the grammar section at the end.

### Example sentence rules

- Example sentences must be taken exactly from the story.
- Example sentences must not repeat across levels - if a verb appears in multiple levels, use a different sentence from the story each time.
- This is important because users will likely listen to all levels in sequence.

### Verb counts per level

- **Just Starting:** 3 verbs, preferably repeated. This is a strict rule, 3 only. Verbs like `come` and `comer` are treated as 2 verbs despite having the same stem.
- **Beginner:** same 3 plus 2 or 3 new ones, max 6 total - but they can repeat.
- **Intermediate:** same verbs from beginner plus 3 or 4 more, max 10 total.
- **Advanced:** 15+ with one or two or three subjunctive verbs.

## Story complexity rules

- At Just Starting and Beginner level, keep sentence structures simple and controlled.
- Try to repeat verbs so the user hears them more often.
- **Just Starting must use exactly 3 verbs, and each verb must keep ONE conjugated form for the whole story.** Different conjugations of the same verb count as separate verbs (`tenía` and `tiene` are two verbs; `lucharon` and `luchó` are two verbs), so do not mix them. To make this easy, **write the entire Just Starting story in a single past tense** (the imperfect works well: `había`, `tenía`, `era`, `estaba`). Describe the situation simply; do NOT narrate a sequence across different time periods and do NOT switch to the present tense (never `ahora Bolivia no tiene...`). A flat, single-tense description like the example is the goal, not a before/now contrast.
- At intermediate and advanced level, join short sentences together using conjunctions like `y`, `pero`, `porque`, `ni`, `que`.
- Short jarringly simple sentences are only appropriate for Just Starting and Beginner.
- The intermediate and advanced stories can use joining phrases like `sin embargo`, `por lo tanto`, `porque`, `de repente`, `a pesar de`, etc.

## Clarity for absolute beginners (Just Starting and Beginner)

- The opening sentence must state the essentials in ONE clear, complete sentence and, when the date is known, it MUST begin with the year: start with `En <año>,` then the main people or countries, what happened, and where. For a war between countries, open exactly like `En 1879, tres países, Chile, Perú y Bolivia, lucharon en el desierto.` (English: "In 1879, three countries, Chile, Peru and Bolivia, fought in the desert.")
- Do NOT scatter those facts across short telegraphic fragments. For example, do NOT write `Tres países. Chile, Perú y Bolivia. Había un desierto.` — combine them into one flowing simple sentence. And never open with an abstract image like `Había un mineral blanco en el desierto`.
- Keep sentences simple, but prefer complete, flowing sentences over one-fact-per-line. Do not lean on `había` for nearly every sentence.
- Never introduce a name, group or term the listener has no context for. Every person or group mentioned must first be introduced in plain words. Do not drop a reference like `Familias aymaras` with no setup — a beginner cannot make sense of it.
- Be concrete and literal; save metaphor for higher levels. The anti-imperialist angle and emotion must come through ordinary, clear sentences a beginner understands, never cryptic fragments.

## Audio and punctuation rule

These stories are recorded as audio. All sections including the English summary must be written so they read naturally when spoken aloud.

- Avoid colons, em dashes, and punctuation that creates ambiguity for a listener.
- Use commas and full stops only.
- Structure sentences so their meaning is clear without visual punctuation cues.
- Use a simple hyphen-dash ( - ) not an em dash ( — ) in all sections that require it.

## Story content rules

- Stories skew anti-imperialist but not heavy-handedly so, as many users will be from the US and Europe.
- Stories cover Latin America including Brazil and Mexico.
- Sources include books like Open Veins of Latin America.
- Information and complexity of the story increases with each level.
- We are providing engaging content for Spanish-learners. Apart from the content being historical we want to write them with emotion, highlighting the tragedies and the injustices.
